## About

I'm a technically grounded Senior Technical Writer with a background in cybersecurity and enterprise systems. I instinctively look for ways to make complex ideas easier to understand and to help people make better use of technology to solve their problems.

I want to understand my audience and what they're trying to accomplish, as well as what problem the technology is meant to solve. I like to see it for myself, exploring how it actually works. Along the way, I investigate assumptions—what it's supposed to do, how it behaves in practice, and whether it really addresses that problem. That understanding shapes both what I write and how I organize it, so the documentation reflects the way users actually work.

I developed this approach through my work at Microsoft and CyberArk, applying it to technologies including SIEM, XDR, privileged access management, and remote access. At Microsoft, I increasingly worked across service and product boundaries, documenting scenarios that required understanding not just individual features but how they worked together to help customers accomplish broader goals. At CyberArk, I led the work to reorganize documentation around user journeys and outcomes rather than the structure of the product. My questions and reviews sometimes led to changes beyond the documentation itself.

Before becoming a technical writer, I spent nearly two decades working in cybersecurity and enterprise infrastructure. That experience means I've often been on the other side of the documentation—as the person trying to understand technology well enough to put it to work. It also lets me approach new technology with context rather than learning each concept in isolation and relate it to the knowledge my audience already brings with them. That same depth has been valuable in mentoring other writers and helping them build their own technical understanding.

I see documentation—and the thoughtful use of AI—as part of a broader objective: helping people get the knowledge they need, when and where they need it, and in a form they can use.

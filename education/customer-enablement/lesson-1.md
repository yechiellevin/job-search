# Customer Enablement — Lesson 1

## Purpose

Establish a working mental model of **Customer Enablement** and determine whether the move from technical writing into customer enablement requires a fundamentally new way of thinking or primarily new methods, tools, and ownership.

## Core definition

Customer Enablement is the systematic work of giving customers the knowledge, skills, guidance, and resources they need to become successful and increasingly self-sufficient users of a product.

The key word is **systematic**.

A technical writer might ask:

> What information does this user need in order to accomplish this task?

A trainer might ask:

> What does this person need to learn, and how should it be taught?

Customer Success might ask:

> Is this customer getting enough value from the product to remain successful?

Customer Enablement connects these questions:

> What should customers learn, when should they learn it, how should that learning be delivered at scale, and how will we know it changed their ability to use the product successfully?

## The conceptual progression

A useful progression is:

**Documentation → Customer Success → Customer Enablement**

This is not necessarily an industry-standard maturity model, but it is a useful way to understand increasing organizational responsibility for customer outcomes.

### Documentation

Primary question:

> Have we given customers the information they need?

The focus is on making knowledge available so users do not need a human to explain the same thing repeatedly.

### Customer Success

Primary question:

> Are customers actually achieving the outcomes they bought the product for?

The function adds proactive human intervention and repeatable practices intended to help customers reach value rather than merely react to support problems.

### Customer Enablement

Primary question:

> Have we systematically equipped customers to achieve those outcomes themselves, at scale?

Enablement takes what is learned through documentation, training, support, and Customer Success interactions and turns as much of it as possible into scalable, repeatable customer capability.

Possible mechanisms include:

- onboarding paths
- help-center content
- live training
- webinars
- short videos
- in-product guidance
- certification programs
- automated communications
- product changes that remove the need for instruction entirely

## The maturity-model insight

The most useful refinement came from viewing this through a **CMMI-style operational maturity lens**.

The progression is not only about each stage having broader responsibility. It is also about steadily converting work that depends on individual human effort and judgment into something that is:

- defined
- repeatable
- standardized
- operationalized
- measurable
- scalable
- potentially automated
- continuously improved

A simple operating loop is:

**Observe → understand → standardize → operationalize → automate → measure → improve**

### Example

Suppose Customer Success Managers repeatedly discover that customers fail at Step 4 of onboarding.

At first, the response may be ad hoc: each Customer Success Manager explains Step 4 personally.

A more mature enablement process would:

1. recognize the recurring pattern;
2. determine why users get stuck;
3. create a standard intervention, such as a guide, video, onboarding checkpoint, webinar segment, or in-product walkthrough;
4. measure whether the intervention improves activation or adoption;
5. refine the intervention based on results;
6. potentially work with Product to remove the underlying friction so the intervention is no longer needed.

This is why the mature endpoint is not always **better enablement content**. Sometimes the best result is **no enablement content**, because the product experience itself has been improved enough to eliminate the need.

## Information → intervention → capability

Another useful way to describe the progression is:

**Information → Intervention → Capability**

- **Documentation** provides information.
- **Customer Success** adds human intervention where needed.
- **Customer Enablement** tries to build lasting customer capability so successful use becomes increasingly self-service and repeatable.

The business effects can then appear downstream as:

**lower support burden → faster adoption → stronger retention → expansion / advocacy**

Retention is therefore better understood as a possible business consequence than as the core object of the maturity model. The deeper object is **customer value realization** or **customer outcomes**.

## Connection to Yechiel's existing way of working

The important conclusion from Lesson 1 is that the conceptual model of Customer Enablement is not foreign.

The underlying habits are already familiar:

- start with what the user is trying to accomplish;
- identify recurring friction and ambiguity;
- understand how the product actually behaves;
- organize information around user journeys and outcomes;
- challenge assumptions;
- turn repeated explanations into reusable structures;
- improve both content and, where possible, the product experience itself.

At CyberArk, reorganizing documentation around user journeys rather than product structure already reflected enablement-style thinking. At Microsoft and CyberArk, hands-on product exploration, training, scenario-based documentation, UX/microcopy work, and collaboration with Product and Customer Success all touched parts of the same system.

The main conceptual expansion is therefore not:

> Learn an entirely new discipline.

It is:

> Stop assuming that documentation is necessarily the solution.

The enablement owner asks how the entire available system — documentation, training, video, in-product guidance, Customer Success intervention, automation, and product design — can help users become successful and self-sufficient.

## What each side brought to the lesson

### Framework introduced in the discussion

The initial model distinguished the roles of Technical Writing, Training, Customer Success, and Customer Enablement and proposed the idea that Enablement connects them through a systematic focus on customer learning, adoption, and measurable success.

It also introduced the idea that the role is broader than documentation because the appropriate intervention may be a guide, webinar, video, tooltip, learning path, direct human intervention, or product change.

### Yechiel's contribution

Yechiel recognized the relationship as a **maturity model**, drawing on prior familiarity with CMMI.

The key addition was that maturity does not merely mean broader scope. It means progressively **institutionalizing capability**: taking work that relies on individuals and turning it into repeatable processes and procedures that increasingly "run by themselves."

That reframing produced the operational loop:

**Observe → understand → standardize → operationalize → automate → measure → improve**

It also made the Tipuli requirement to "build an enablement function from the ground up" much more concrete: study how customers currently learn and adopt the product, identify repeated human work and recurring friction, turn those into scalable interventions, measure them, and iterate.

## Lesson 1 conclusion

**Conceptual leap accomplished — or, more accurately, already built in.**

The fundamental way of thinking required for Customer Enablement is highly compatible with Yechiel's existing systems-oriented approach.

The remaining question is not whether the conceptual foundation exists. It is which parts of the actual **enablement machinery** are already familiar, which are analogous to prior experience, and which represent genuine gaps.

That is the subject of Lesson 2.
